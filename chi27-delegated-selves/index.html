Overview

AI agents are becoming social actors, not just private tools.

AI agents increasingly draft and send messages, participate in group spaces, negotiate on behalf of people, make disclosures, and maintain persistent public personas. These capabilities create a distinct privacy and security problem: an agent's action can expose information about a user, a relationship, or an entire group, while the agent's own identity and provenance may remain ambiguous.

This workshop brings together HCI, privacy, security, AI, and social computing researchers and practitioners to develop a shared agenda for designing and governing social AI agents.

Workshop Scope

Four lenses for delegated social AI

The workshop frames social AI agents as delegated actors embedded in relationships, groups, communities, and institutions.

1

Relational delegation and group privacy

How should privacy work when an agent's action may disclose another person's information, reveal a relationship, or shift a group's boundary?

2

Security of social action

How do prompt injection, spoofing, malicious delegation, memory poisoning, broad permissions, and unsafe tool use become social harms?

3

Disclosure, provenance, and social identity

What forms of labels, signatures, avatars, histories, or provenance records help recipients interpret agent-mediated communication?

4

The agent as a social actor

When is an agent treated as a tool, proxy, teammate, community member, or public persona, and how does that redistribute responsibility?

Call for Participation

We invite position papers and design provocations.

Submissions should address privacy, security, identity, accountability, or governance challenges raised by social AI agents acting on behalf of people in shared social settings.

Contact Organizers

  • Delegated consent and accountability
  • Prompt injection, impersonation, and memory poisoning
  • Unauthorized disclosure and provenance denial

Extended abstracts of up to 4 pages, excluding references, prepared with the ACM Master Article Template. Accepted papers will be non-archival.

Review criteria

Relevance, quality, potential to stimulate discussion, and diversity of perspectives.

Important dates

Submission deadline, notification, and camera-ready dates will be announced.

Workshop Structure

Two collaborative sessions built around shared artifacts

Activities emphasize discussion, design, red-teaming, and synthesis rather than a sequence of long talks.

Session 1 · 80 min

Understanding social agents' risks

  1. Welcome and introduction · 5 min
  2. Keynote · 30 min · speaker to be announced
  3. Lightning provocations · 15 min
  4. Paper presentations · 30 min · six short talks
Break

Networking and regrouping

Participants prepare for collaborative scenario work.

Session 2 · 80 min

Designing accountable agents

  1. Group discussion · 20 min · red-team a social-agent scenario
  2. Design sketches · 20 min · propose concrete interventions
  3. Cross-group evaluation · 15 min · structured feedback
  4. Group presentations · 20 min · refined designs
  5. Synthesis and closing · 5 min · next steps

Participation

Inclusive discussion by design

Facilitators will use prepared worksheets, visible timeboxes, rotating reporters, and an anonymous question channel. Participants may pass on any scenario. No participant data will be collected for research during the workshop.
